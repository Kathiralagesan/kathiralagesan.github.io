Infection Glycobiology

Systems Glycoproteomics & Host-Pathogen Interactions

Glycosylation is ubiquitous, yet its roles in host-pathogen interactions remain one of the least understood areas in molecular biology. We are changing that.

We develop and apply high-end analytics to decipher the complex "glycocode" — decoding the structural diversity and functional implications of glycoconjugates produced by both host and pathogen.

Core Research Focus

The Human GlycoAtlas Initiative

Defining the baseline of human glycosylation is essential for understanding infection. We are comprehensively mapping site-specific N- and O-glycosylation across human tissues and plasma to establish a definitive reference framework — elucidating the precise receptor topologies that pathogens exploit for adhesion, invasion, and immune evasion.

Infection Glycodynamics

Pathogens, including S. pyogenes, exploit highly specific glycan-lectin interactions to adhere, invade, and evade host immunity. We deploy advanced glycoproteomics to capture dynamic alterations in both host and bacterial glycoproteomes during infection — uncovering the molecular dialects that dictate virulence and inflammatory responses.

Glycoconjugate Vaccine Engineering

Translating fundamental glycobiology into clinical therapeutics requires absolute structural certainty. By leveraging our next-generation MS analytical platforms, we guide the development of recombinant glycoconjugate vaccines for Group A Streptococcus.

We provide rigorous validation of in vivo carrier protein glycosylation — analyzing complex glycan heterogeneity, resolving isomeric structures, and confirming precise site occupancy. Our FAIMS-assisted nanoLC-MS/MS workflows detected glycopeptides containing up to 41 rhamnose residues with full structural confirmation.
