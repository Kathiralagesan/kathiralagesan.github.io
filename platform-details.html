Proteomics Research Platform

Max Planck Unit for the Science of Pathogens · Berlin, Germany

Microbial Proteomics enabled by innovations in Chemical Biology, Mass Spectrometry & Glycoproteome Informatics

A research platform that operates at the intersection of science and service — pursuing independent discovery whilst empowering institute-wide research through state-of-the-art analytical infrastructure.

The Why

We believe that unlocking the molecular language of host-pathogen interactions demands more than standard tools. Our purpose is to build a unique, integrative microbial proteomic map — one that decodes how bacteria respond to, exploit, and reshape the host proteome, glycoproteome, and beyond. Every workflow we develop, every dataset we curate, and every collaboration we pursue is in service of this singular goal: transforming mass spectrometry data into biological insight with the highest possible depth, reproducibility, and openness.

The How

Our Approach

From the very first experimental consultation through to final data deposition in public repositories, we apply a rigorous, project-tailored framework. Our approach integrates three interconnected pillars — analytical excellence, systems thinking, and open science — that collectively define how we generate, interpret, and share proteomics knowledge.

▸ Chemical Biology & Sample Preparation

We implement the next-generation SP3 (Single-Pot Solid-Phase-enhanced) sample preparation technology, delivering unmatched sensitivity for low-abundance and low-volume samples. For larger cohorts, we deploy the Agilent Bravo robotic liquid-handling platform, enabling high-throughput, minimally variable proteomics at scale. Specialist expertise covers:

  • Multi-protease digestion (trypsin, LysC, chymotrypsin, ArgC, GluC, AspN) for enhanced sequence coverage
  • Stable isotope labeling (SILAC, TMT, dimethyl) for relative and absolute quantification
  • HILIC-based enrichment of N- and O-glycopeptides
  • TiO₂/Ti-IMAC enrichment of phosphopeptides
  • Peptide-SEC for cross-linked peptide analysis
  • High-pH reverse-phase pre-fractionation for deep proteome coverage

▸ Mass Spectrometry & Fragmentation

All LC-MS/MS workflows are executed on dual high-resolution hybrid Orbitrap platforms coupled to FAIMS PRO ion mobility sources, providing superior analyte selectivity and expanded proteome depth. Our MS expertise spans:

  • Data-Dependent (DDA) and Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA) at system-wide scale
  • EThcD and ETD fragmentation for intact glycopeptide characterization
  • HCD, CID and beam-type activation for phosphopeptides and standard proteomics
  • FAIMS-assisted nanoLC-MS/MS for improved PTM detection in complex backgrounds
  • Targeted workflows (Skyline-based PRM/MRM) for consistent quantification of pre-selected targets
  • Analysis of protein-protein, protein-RNA, and protein-DNA interaction networks

▸ Proteome Informatics & Visualization

We operate a bespoke, modular informatics ecosystem — SILAMBU — that covers end-to-end proteomics data processing and reporting. In-house tools include VizGlyProt for glycoproteomics knowledge base construction and pathway visualization, and Magnum Peptide Investigator for interactive protein sequence coverage analysis. Software deployed includes:

  • MSFragger & FragPipe for high-throughput database searching
  • Spectronaut for DIA analysis with predicted spectral libraries
  • Byonic for complex intact glycopeptide identification
  • MaxQuant / Perseus for label-based quantification and statistical analysis
  • R/Bioconductor custom pipelines for differential expression, GSEA, and multi-omics integration
  • Interactive HTML dashboards for reproducible reporting

▸ FAIR Data & Open Science

Every dataset generated by PROT is handled according to the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and the MIPAGE (Minimum Information About a Proteomics Experiment) community standard. All LC-MS/MS raw data are deposited in the ProteomeXchange / PRIDE repository prior to manuscript submission, and data analysis pipelines are versioned and documented for full reproducibility. As an Open Science Ambassador for the Max Planck Society since 2024, Dr. Alagesan is actively committed to fostering a culture of transparency, knowledge sharing, and open infrastructure across the MPUSP community and beyond.

The What

Infrastructure & Services

Two state-of-the-art mass spectrometers. A full analytical pipeline from sample to insight. Available to MPUSP researchers, external collaborators, and partner institutions.

Orbitrap Exploris™ 480

A versatile, high-resolution benchtop mass spectrometer optimized for rapid, deep quantitative proteomics workflows. Ideal for large-cohort DDA/DIA experiments requiring maximum throughput and reproducibility.

  • High-resolution MS/MS at scan rates optimized for DIA
  • FAIMS PRO interface for improved selectivity in complex matrices
  • Coupled to Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC in nano configuration

Orbitrap Fusion™ Lumos Tribrid

A top-of-the-range tribrid platform combining the Orbitrap, ion trap, and quadrupole mass analyzers with full ETD capability. The instrument of choice for challenging glycopeptide and phosphopeptide analyses requiring EThcD or multistage fragmentation.

  • EThcD, ETD, HCD, and CID fragmentation modes
  • FAIMS PRO source for atmospheric ion mobility
  • Coupled to Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC in nano and capLC configuration

Chromatography Suite

A complete chromatography infrastructure for peptide separations at every scale — from nano-amounts to preparative fractionation.

  • Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC × 2 — nano and capLC, online desalting
  • Agilent 1260 Infinity II HPLC — high-pH reverse phase, SEC, ion exchange
  • HILIC glycopeptide enrichment and TiO₂/Ti-IMAC phosphopeptide enrichment

Automated Sample Processing

Scalable, reproducible sample preparation for high-throughput studies and clinical cohorts.

  • Agilent Bravo robotic liquid-handling platform
  • SP3 and Mag-Resyn® workflows for minimal-loss protein digestion
  • Standardised QC protocols with Pierce MS-compatible standards
  • Fully documented SOP library for reproducibility and inter-lab transfer

Full Service Menu

Pre-Experimental Consultation

Scientific advice on experimental design, including sample size calculations, appropriate controls, and protease strategy selection. We work with you before a single tube is opened to maximize the return on your experiment.

Quantitative Proteomics

Discovery-driven and targeted quantification workflows — from label-free and dimethyl labeling to SILAC and TMT — for single proteins up to whole-cell lysates and secretome samples.

Glycoproteomics

Site-specific N- and O-glycopeptide identification and relative quantification using HILIC enrichment, FAIMS-assisted nanoLC-MS/MS, and Byonic-based intact glycopeptide searching. Automated N/O-glycosylation pathway reconstruction via VizGlyProt.

PTM Analysis

Phosphoproteomics (pSer, pThr, pTyr), arginine phosphorylation, and broad-spectrum PTM identification at the site-specific level. Multi-PTM and PTM crosstalk analysis for complex regulatory questions.

Interactomics

Protein-protein, protein-RNA (RIP/CLIP-seq adjacent workflows), and protein-DNA interaction partner identification by co-IP followed by LC-MS/MS. Cross-linking MS (XL-MS) analysis pipeline (internal service).

Data Analysis & Reporting

Full bioinformatics support: peptide/protein identification, quantification, differential expression testing, pathway enrichment (GSEA), clustering, and biological interpretation. Delivered as interactive HTML notebooks and structured reports.

Impact in Action

Selected Contributions

Since its establishment in 2019, PROT has contributed to nearly every research project across the three MPUSP laboratories, demonstrating that deep, service-oriented proteomics and independent research can genuinely coexist.

NPJ Vaccines · 2025

Glycoconjugate Vaccine for S. pyogenes

In collaboration with Helge C. Dorfmueller (University of Dundee), PROT provided the glycoproteomics backbone for a landmark Nature NPJ Vaccines paper developing a recombinant GAS glycoconjugate vaccine. Using FAIMS-assisted nanoLC-MS/MS and Byonic, the platform detected glycopeptides carrying up to 41 rhamnose residues with full structural confirmation — a technical achievement at the outer boundary of intact glycopeptide MS. Site-specific glycosylation was confirmed for both NanA and IdeS carrier proteins, directly informing immunogenicity design.

Community Benchmark · Nat Methods 2021

Glycoproteomics Informatics Benchmark

As a participant in the international community evaluation of glycoproteomics search strategies (Kawahara et al., Nature Methods 2021), PROT contributed to establishing high-performance analytical benchmarks for serum glycopeptide analysis. The study, covering 17 different software solutions, positioned PROT's FAIMS-enhanced workflow among the highest-performing strategies for intact glycopeptide identification.

Ongoing · S. pyogenes Systems Biology

Expanding the Bacterial Proteome Landscape

Using optimized DIA with Spectronaut and a predicted spectral library, PROT has achieved identification of 75% (1,300 of 1,690 ORFs) of the S. pyogenes M1 reference proteome across three growth phases. This work forms the foundation for the ongoing investigation of how S. pyogenes modifies, exploits, and decorates host glycoproteins when exposed to human ABO plasma — linking the bacterium's virulence strategy directly to glycan biology.

Multi-Lab Support · 2019–Present

Institute-Wide Proteomics Infrastructure

PROT has provided LC-MS-based bioanalytical services to over 30 scientists across MPUSP, the Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology (MPIIB), and the Deutsches Rheuma-Forschungszentrum (DRFZ). This includes proteomic contributions to published studies on streptococcal EVs and inflammasome activation (EMBO Rep, 2025), RNA-binding protein translation regulation (Nat Commun, 2025), and Drosophila infection dynamics (PNAS, 2025 — in press).

Looking Forward

Research Horizon

The overarching objective of PROT is to build the most comprehensive integrative proteomic map of microbial pathogenesis and host-microbe glycobiology yet assembled. The following strategic directions define our research programme for the coming years, combining methodological innovation with bold biological questions.

ABO Blood Group Glycosylation & Host-Pathogen Interaction

The ABO blood group system — one of the most biologically fundamental glycan systems in humans — defines the molecular fingerprint of the host glycocalyx. We are actively investigating how S. pyogenes senses, exploits, and potentially remodels ABO-type glycans on host plasma proteins to gain a competitive advantage during infection. This work integrates our GlycoAtlas baseline datasets (site-specific N- and O-glycosylation of 10 human tissue lysates and ABO blood group plasma) with infection models to directly map glycan-mediated host-pathogen crosstalk at the molecular level.

Thermal Proteome Profiling for Novel Drug Target Discovery

Thermal proteome profiling (TPP) / protein thermal stability (Proteome Integral Solubility Alteration — PISA) offers a powerful, unbiased approach to identify drug-protein interactions and protein stability changes at the proteome scale. We are developing TPP workflows optimized for bacterial proteomes — which differ substantially from mammalian cells in protein abundance distributions and solubility characteristics — to identify novel anti-infective targets in S. pyogenes and B. subtilis.

PTM Crosstalk & Multi-Modification Proteomics

Post-translational modifications rarely operate in isolation. Phosphorylation, glycosylation, arginine phosphorylation, and ubiquitination can compete for the same residue or regulate each other through downstream signaling cascades. Using multi-protease strategies (enabling near-complete sequence coverage) combined with multi-enrichment workflows, we are systematically mapping PTM crosstalk in bacterial stress responses — particularly how arginine phosphorylation and glycosylation together govern protein stability and function in Gram-positive pathogens.

Small Proteins & Their Interactomes

Proteins of fewer than 100 amino acids — so-called "small proteins" or "sProteins" — are dramatically underrepresented in standard proteomics workflows, due to their poor retention on C18 columns and high charge-state complexity. We are developing dedicated analytical strategies (alternative fractionation, alternative proteases, alternative enrichment) for sProtein identification and characterization in S. pyogenes, with the goal of uncovering hidden regulatory networks that govern the transition between growth phases and virulence states.

Phage Infection Dynamics & Bacterial Defense Proteomics

Bacteriophages are increasingly recognized as key drivers of bacterial evolution, virulence, and antibiotic resistance. PROT will establish quantitative proteomics workflows to monitor the dynamic proteome remodeling that occurs in bacteria during phage infection — from initial adsorption through replication to lysis. This programme will provide insight into both phage biology and the bacterial defense mechanisms it triggers, with potential implications for phage therapy strategies.
